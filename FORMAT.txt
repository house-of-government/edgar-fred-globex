FASTA
=====

Purpose
-------
FASTA is the intentionally simple biological text lane. It is useful both as a parser fixture and as a fixed-string-search workload because sequence data is mostly long runs of A/C/G/T (or protein/IUPAC symbols) separated by short record headers.

Core grammar
------------
- A record begins with `>` at the beginning of a line.
- The rest of that line is the record title/description.
- One or more following lines contain sequence symbols until the next `>` record header or end of file.
- Sequence lines are commonly wrapped but wrapping is not semantically meaningful.
- Files may contain one or many records.

Common real-world complications
-------------------------------
- identifiers plus free-form descriptions in headers;
- duplicate identifiers;
- CRLF versus LF line endings;
- arbitrarily wrapped versus unwrapped sequence lines;
- nucleotide, RNA, protein and ambiguous IUPAC alphabets;
- long records where fixed motif search should not require building a whole syntax tree;
- record-boundary policy: a motif must not silently match across two distinct records.

Parser/search questions
-----------------------
- detect headers only at line boundaries;
- distinguish header bytes from sequence bytes;
- skip line breaks during sequence assembly/search if the search semantics say wrapped sequence is continuous;
- preserve record boundaries;
- permit exact fixed-string scanning over sequence data without accidentally scanning descriptions unless requested;
- decide explicitly whether lower/upper case is significant.

Samples
-------
`sample-small.fasta`: three short DNA reads.
`sample-complex.fasta`: one substantially longer wrapped nucleotide record with a rich identifier/description and CRLF-origin source.

Useful first acceptance
-----------------------
Search a literal DNA motif in `sample-small.fasta`, report exact record and sequence offset, then prove no match can cross a `>` record boundary.
